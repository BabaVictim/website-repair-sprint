# Public Website Preflight Report

## Draft Scope and Terms

**Draft notice:** This is a practical starting point, not legal advice. The
provider and customer should adapt it for their locations, tax obligations, and
the facts of the engagement. An individually accepted proposal controls if it
conflicts with this draft.

### 1. Service

The preflight is a read-only review of one existing, publicly reachable website.
It covers the homepage and up to two additional public HTTP(S) pages agreed
before payment. No account, credential, source-code access, or production change
is required.

The report may cover:

- Visible layout and responsive behavior at representative viewport sizes.
- Navigation, links, calls to action, and form usability without submitting data.
- Keyboard access and practical accessibility heuristics.
- Page titles, descriptions, canonical links, social metadata, and indexing hints.
- Public response status, redirects, common security headers, and bounded
  performance observations.
- A prioritized list of observed problems, evidence, and suggested next steps.

The report is a time-limited observation, not a guarantee of completeness,
security, compliance, search ranking, revenue, uptime, or future behavior.

### 2. Boundaries

The preflight does not:

- Log in, bypass access controls, submit forms, create accounts, or alter data.
- Probe ports, exploit vulnerabilities, perform penetration testing, or claim to
  be a security assessment.
- Crawl the whole site or test pages outside the accepted list.
- Review private code, customer records, analytics, hosting, or internal systems.
- Include remediation, deployment, a pull request, or ongoing monitoring.

Unexpected sensitive information or a suspected security issue will not be
posted publicly. Work may stop while the parties agree on a safe channel.

### 3. Authority and intake

The customer represents that it owns the website or has authority from the owner
to request the review. The public intake issue must contain only a public URL and
non-sensitive symptoms. Private details may be exchanged later through a
customer-controlled temporary private repository or another mutually agreed
channel.

### 4. Price, invoice, and start

The introductory fixed price is **$49 USD, paid as the BTC equivalent**. The
customer pays 100% before work begins.

The written confirmation and invoice state:

- The included URLs and report priorities.
- The USD and BTC amounts.
- The exchange-rate source and exact rate timestamp.
- The mainnet payment address, invoice expiration, required confirmations, and
  responsibility for network fees.
- The delivery channel and expected delivery time.

No payment should be sent from a public tip link or before the individualized
scope and invoice are confirmed. If an invoice expires unpaid, a replacement may
use a new exchange rate.

### 5. Delivery

Delivery is targeted within one working day after the agreed payment has the
required confirmations and the public pages remain reachable. The report is
normally delivered as Markdown through the agreed channel.

The schedule may pause if the target is unavailable, blocks bounded requests,
changes materially, or requires access outside the accepted scope. If the review
cannot reasonably begin, the parties will agree on a replacement target,
reschedule, or refund, subject to applicable law and any work already authorized
and performed.

### 6. Follow-up work

The customer may use the report independently. Any repair, code access,
deployment, expanded review, or recurring monitoring requires a separate written
scope and price. Purchasing a preflight does not require purchasing the repair
sprint.

### 7. Confidentiality and publication

The provider will not publish the customer’s report, screenshots, name, logo,
metrics, or testimonial without separate written permission. A customer who
selects public issue delivery expressly requests public delivery only for the
agreed non-sensitive report.
