# 48-Hour Website Repair Sprint

## Draft Service Scope and Terms

**Draft notice:** This document is a practical starting point, not legal advice. It should be reviewed and adapted for the provider's location, the customer's location, tax obligations, and the facts of each engagement. If a signed proposal conflicts with this draft, the signed proposal controls for that engagement.

### 1. Service summary

The 48-Hour Website Repair Sprint is a focused repair engagement for one existing website. Its purpose is to resolve a short, agreed list of high-priority website defects within a scheduled 48-hour work window.

The sprint is not a promise of business results, revenue, search ranking, traffic, uninterrupted operation, or complete security. Outcomes depend on the website, hosting, third-party services, timely customer decisions, and the agreed scope.

### 2. Typical included work

The signed proposal must name the exact included tasks. Depending on the site and diagnosis, a sprint may include:

- Repairing broken layout, navigation, forms, links, or responsive behavior.
- Correcting a small number of accessibility, performance, or technical SEO defects.
- Resolving an isolated theme, plugin, dependency, or configuration conflict.
- Improving an existing page or conversion path without replacing the full site.
- Deploying the approved fixes and documenting the work.
- One consolidated round of minor corrections that directly relate to the agreed repairs.

Only work listed under “Included scope” in the accepted proposal is included.

### 3. Normally excluded work

Unless expressly added to the signed proposal, the following are excluded:

- A new website, full redesign, rebrand, or open-ended development.
- New copywriting, photography, illustration, paid assets, or large-scale content entry.
- E-commerce migrations, payment-account setup, custom back-end systems, or database recovery.
- Penetration testing, incident response, malware eradication, regulatory certification, or a security guarantee.
- Search-ranking, sales, traffic, uptime, or performance guarantees.
- Work on any account, system, data, or website the customer does not own or have authority to administer.
- Third-party fees, licenses, hosting, domains, advertising, taxes, or transaction/network fees.

New findings are documented and may be proposed separately. They do not silently expand the sprint.

### 4. Start conditions and the 48-hour window

The work window begins only after all of the following are complete:

1. Both parties accept the individualized proposal and included task list.
2. The customer completes the intake and gives written authorization for access and changes.
3. Required access is tested successfully through an agreed secure channel.
4. A usable backup or rollback method is confirmed.
5. The 50% upfront payment is received with sufficient blockchain confirmations.
6. Both parties confirm the start date and time in writing.

The 48 hours are elapsed hours, including nights and weekends, unless the proposal says otherwise. The provider will use reasonable efforts to deliver during that window. The schedule pauses for customer-caused delays, missing access, unavailable third-party services, new safety concerns, or requested scope changes. A pause is not a guarantee that every discovered issue can be repaired.

### 5. Price and BTC payment

The introductory fixed price is **$300 USD, paid as the BTC equivalent**:

- **50% upfront:** $150 USD equivalent in BTC, due before scheduling begins.
- **50% on acceptance:** $150 USD equivalent in BTC, due when the work is accepted under Section 8.

Each invoice should state:

- The USD amount.
- The BTC amount.
- The named exchange-rate source and exact rate timestamp.
- The payment address, network, expiration time, and required confirmations.
- Who pays blockchain transaction fees.

The BTC amount is locked for the invoice validity period stated on that invoice. If it expires unpaid, a replacement invoice may use a new market rate. The provider should verify the destination address with the customer through the agreed channel; cryptocurrency transfers are generally difficult or impossible to reverse. The provider does not provide investment, trading, tax, or custody advice.

Unless the proposal says otherwise, the customer pays network fees and legally applicable taxes. The provider supplies any invoice or receipt required by applicable law.

### 6. Customer authority and authorization

The customer represents that it owns the website and related accounts or has permission from the owner to authorize the work.

By accepting the proposal and signing the intake authorization, the customer expressly authorizes the provider, only for the engagement and agreed scope, to:

- Access the listed website, hosting, domain, content-management, repository, analytics, and related accounts.
- Create a backup or staging copy where technically appropriate.
- Inspect configuration, logs, code, content, and settings reasonably necessary for the repairs.
- Modify, test, publish, and if necessary roll back the specifically approved changes.
- Use approved third-party tools only when identified in the proposal or separately approved in writing.

Authorization does not extend to unrelated accounts, data, websites, or systems. The customer may revoke authorization in writing, but revocation may stop delivery and does not erase payment obligations for authorized work already performed.

The customer remains responsible for account ownership, lawful content, privacy notices, data-processing instructions, and securing any internal approval needed for the changes.

### 7. Access, credentials, backups, and data

- Credentials must be shared through an agreed secure method, not placed in a proposal, ordinary email thread, or this document.
- The customer should create time-limited, least-privilege accounts where possible.
- The provider will use access only for the authorized work and will not intentionally retain credentials after handoff.
- The customer should revoke temporary accounts and rotate shared credentials after delivery.
- The parties will identify the backup/rollback owner before work starts.
- The provider will minimize access to personal or confidential data. If sensitive data or a suspected compromise is discovered, work may stop while the parties agree on a safe, lawful next step.

The service is not a substitute for a separate backup, cybersecurity, privacy, or compliance program.

### 8. Review and acceptance

The delivery report will list completed work, tests, known limitations, and any customer actions. The customer will review the delivery against the proposal's written acceptance criteria.

The customer should either:

- Accept the work in writing; or
- Send one consolidated written list of specific in-scope defects within **two business days** of delivery.

The provider will make reasonable corrections for verified defects caused by the delivered in-scope work. Requests that add features, alter approved direction, or concern pre-existing/out-of-scope conditions require a new quote.

If the customer does not provide acceptance or a specific in-scope defect list within two business days, the work may be treated as accepted to the extent permitted by applicable law and the signed proposal. The final 50% payment is then due. This review period is an administrative acceptance process, not a guarantee that no later defect can exist.

### 9. Changes, cancellation, and refunds

Any scope, price, or schedule change must be agreed in writing. The provider may decline unsafe, unlawful, unauthorized, deceptive, or materially out-of-scope instructions.

Before the scheduled start, either party may request cancellation. The proposal should state whether and how much of the upfront payment is refundable, taking into account work already performed, reserved time, non-refundable fees, and applicable consumer law. After work begins, the provider may invoice authorized work performed up to cancellation. No clause in this draft overrides mandatory rights under applicable law.

### 10. Intellectual property and third-party materials

After full payment, the customer receives the provider's rights in the custom repair work created specifically for the engagement, except for:

- Pre-existing provider tools, templates, know-how, and reusable components.
- Open-source or third-party materials governed by their own licenses.
- Customer or third-party content the provider did not own.

The provider grants the customer a continuing license to any embedded pre-existing provider material needed to use the delivered repair, unless the proposal specifies different terms. The customer is responsible for rights to the content and assets it supplies.

### 11. Confidentiality and publicity

Each party should use reasonable care with non-public information received for the engagement and use it only for the work or related recordkeeping. Legal disclosure obligations may still apply.

The provider may not publish the customer's name, logo, screenshots, metrics, testimonial, or case study without separate written permission. No testimonial or endorsement is implied by purchasing the sprint.

### 12. Warranties, risk, and responsibility

The provider will perform the agreed work with reasonable care and skill. Websites depend on changing browsers, hosting, plugins, APIs, and other third parties, so defects or incompatibilities may still occur.

No result is guaranteed beyond any non-excludable obligation under applicable law. Any proposed exclusions, liability limits, dispute terms, governing law, or consumer-rights language should be reviewed by qualified local counsel before use; none is asserted by this draft beyond what applicable law permits.

The engagement record should include the accepted proposal, completed intake authorization, payment invoices and transaction references, delivery report, acceptance or defect notice, and any approved changes.
